01 — CONTEXT

Accountable all the way to the drop point

Under Presidential Regulation No. 6/2025 on subsidized fertilizer governance — and its implementing Ministry of Agriculture Regulation No. 15/2025 — Pupuk Indonesia carries full responsibility for subsidized fertilizer until it reaches the drop point: farmer groups, retailers and cooperatives.

Fertilizer gets there in one of two ways: through appointed distribution partners, or directly from producer to drop point under a sale-and-purchase agreement. Either way, every shipment has to be accounted for.

iDelivery is the web platform distribution partners use to assign deliveries and follow each shipment’s status — from the producer’s warehouse to the drop point.

02 — MY ROLE

Building a temple overnight

In Indonesian folklore, a thousand temples had to rise in a single night. iDelivery felt a little like that: the platform was needed fast, and there was no settled business process yet from stakeholders or users to build it on.

So the job went well beyond writing an API. I helped shape the process itself, then turned it into data and rules the system could enforce.

  1. 1

    Business process

    Mapped how a delivery should move, from assignment to drop point, together with stakeholders — before any of it existed on paper.

  2. 2

    Database design

    Designed the data structure that every shipment, warehouse and partner record lives in.

  3. 3

    Business rules

    Wrote down what is and isn’t allowed at each step, so the system enforces the process instead of trusting it.

  4. 4

    The API

    Built the backend that brings it all together — the part people usually assume is the whole job.

THE CATCH

Fast and undefined is a risky combination.

With subsidized fertilizer on the line, every rule doubled as a safeguard. Risk mitigation had to be strong enough to hold under real deliveries, and accurate enough to match how distribution actually works on the ground.
